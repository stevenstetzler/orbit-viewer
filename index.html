Orbit Viewer

An interactive solar system and orbit viewer in your browser, built on the JPL DE440 planetary ephemeris and read directly from NAIF SPK kernels. Pick a view below — each is a different way of looking at the same real data.

Explore

Solar System

/solar-system/

The Sun and nine planets, seen from the Sun, drawn as idealized orbit ellipses. Scrub the epoch, and add any asteroid or comet by name via JPL Horizons.

Solar System Trajectories

/solar-system/trajectory/

The same view, but every orbit is the real sampled path rather than an ellipse. Re-center on any body, or upload your own .bsp trajectory.

Close Approaches

/close-approach/

Every real asteroid and comet pass within 2 lunar distances of Earth since 1900. Click a row to jump to that approach and see its actual trajectory past Earth and the Moon.

Full Explorer

/examples/browser-demo/

The complete demo with every control exposed: frames, rotating views, scale modes, custom kernels, and live Horizons search. The most powerful, and the busiest.

Planets and their moons

About the data

Positions come from the JPL DE440 ephemeris, covering 1900–2100 in these views. Kernels stream on demand over HTTP range requests, so only the bytes a view needs are fetched. Asteroid, comet, and spacecraft trajectories come from JPL Horizons; close-approach events come from JPL's Close-Approach Data API.

Source and docs on GitHub. Built on spiceJS. This application has been created with AI, primarily with Claude Code.
